## What Does an SDR Do in B2B Sales?

An SDR (Sales Development Representative) is a B2B sales role focused on qualifying **inbound** leads — prospects who already showed interest via a demo request, content download, or website form. Their core job is to assess fit, qualify the opportunity, and hand off ready-to-buy prospects to an Account Executive, filtering out cold interest before it reaches senior sales talent.

According to HubSpot, "a sales development representative (SDR) is a specialized sales professional who focuses on the early stages of the sales pipeline by identifying, qualifying, and nurturing potential customers before passing them to closing representatives."

That definition captures a key nuance: the SDR role is not just about making calls, it's about qualifying and nurturing before the handoff. "Identifying" here usually means identifying the specific need within an existing inbound inquiry, not hunting for new accounts from scratch. The SDR works the prospects who already raised a hand.

### Core SDR Responsibilities

The day-to-day reality of an SDR is defined by speed, precision, and rigorous qualification. Unlike full-cycle sales roles, the SDR's primary metric isn't revenue closed — it's qualified meetings booked for the Account Executive (AE).

- **Researching accounts:** Before engaging an inbound lead, the SDR reviews the prospect's company, role, and recent activity to tailor the discovery conversation, so the AE receives a lead who is both interested and a structural fit.
- **Discovery calls:** The SDR runs short, focused conversations to uncover pain points, budget signals, authority, and timeline — validating interest rather than solving complex problems.
- **Qualifying with frameworks:** Most mature sales organizations use frameworks like BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timing) or MEDDIC to standardize qualification, so every AE receives consistent, comparable data.
- **Scheduling AE meetings:** The output of an SDR's day is a qualified meeting on an AE's calendar, which requires selling the prospect on the value of the next conversation.
- **Tracking engagement data:** SDRs log interactions in the CRM carefully. This data feeds marketing attribution and helps AEs prepare for handoffs — see how [content marketing strategies for B2B companies](https://marketing.growthgear.com.au/content-marketing/best-content-marketing-strategies-b2b-companies) generate the inbound pipeline SDRs qualify in the first place.

### How SDRs Are Measured

SDR performance is highly quantifiable because their work is the first step in the pipeline. Common KPIs include call and email volume as a leading indicator, contact-to-opportunity conversion rate as a measure of qualification skill, meeting-to-opportunity ratio (a low ratio means the SDR is booking meetings with unqualified prospects), and response time to inbound leads, where speed is critical.

### SDRs vs. Inside Sales Representatives

It's worth distinguishing SDRs from full-cycle Inside Sales Representatives. An Inside Sales Rep manages the entire cycle from first contact to close: they prospect, qualify, present, negotiate, and close. An SDR specializes only in the top of the funnel and doesn't run the cycle through to close, which lets them build deeper expertise in qualification and pipeline generation while AEs focus on closing. For a broader look at how these roles fit into a full sales org, see our [B2B inside sales guide](/b2b-sales/b2b-inside-sales-guide).

An SDR's success is defined by the quality of the handoff, not the final contract. They are the gatekeepers of an AE's time, and that separation of duties is a hallmark of scalable B2B sales organizations.

## What Does a BDR Do in B2B Sales?

A BDR (Business Development Representative) is a B2B sales role focused on **outbound** prospecting — proactively identifying and cold-contacting companies that have never engaged with the business, creating pipeline where none existed. The core distinction from an SDR is demand creation versus demand response: an SDR reacts to interest, a BDR generates it.

According to Salesforce, "a business development representative (BDR) is the sales position responsible for generating new business opportunities for their organization." That mandate implies a proactive stance: the BDR doesn't wait for a form fill. They identify a target account, research the decision-makers, and initiate contact cold, which demands a different profile than the SDR role — typically higher resilience and tolerance for a longer path to a first conversation.

### The Nuance of Role Definitions

Sales development terminology is not standardized across the industry. Salesforce's own article notes these titles "are often used interchangeably in the sales world, but sometimes have distinct roles within specific organizations."

Some companies assign inbound work to BDRs and outbound to SDRs — the reverse of the dominant convention. Others use BDR as an entry-level title before promoting to SDR. There is no single industry standard, so always check a specific company's actual job description rather than assume from the title alone. That said, the prevailing convention at most mature tech and SaaS companies is BDR for outbound, SDR for inbound.

### Core BDR Responsibilities

The BDR's daily workflow centers on building a pipeline from cold prospects.

- **Market and account research:** BDRs identify target industries, company sizes, and pain points, building lists of prospects who fit the Ideal Customer Profile but haven't engaged yet.
- **Cold email and phone campaigns:** BDRs design and run multi-channel outreach sequences — personalized cold emails, cold calls, and LinkedIn touches. See our [outbound lead generation guide](/sales-techniques/outbound-lead-generation-guide) for how BDRs structure this work.
- **CRM documentation:** Accurate logging of outreach attempts and responses lets the team see which accounts are being pursued and the status of each one.
- **Scheduling discovery calls:** Like SDRs, BDRs aim to book AE meetings, though these often need more education to move a prospect from unaware to interested.
- **Collaboration with marketing:** BDRs feed back which messaging resonates in cold outreach and may help shape outbound-specific content like case studies.

### The BDR-to-AE Career Path

Salesforce notes the BDR role is often the entry rung before Account Executive, giving new hires a structured way to learn the sales craft without the immediate pressure of closing. BDRs build foundational skills in prospecting, objection handling, and CRM discipline, then may move to SDR — if the company splits the roles — or directly to AE. ## SDR vs BDR: Key Differences at a Glance

SDRs and BDRs both sit at the top of the funnel and both hand qualified prospects to Account Executives, but the core difference is lead source and motion: SDRs react to inbound interest, BDRs manufacture outbound interest from scratch. That single distinction drives the skills required, the daily workflow, and how each role is measured.

| Dimension | SDR | BDR |
|---|---|---|
| Lead source | Inbound (demo requests, content downloads, form fills) | Outbound (cold accounts, no prior engagement) |
| Core motion | Qualify existing interest | Create new interest |
| Primary channels | Follow-up calls/emails on warm leads | Cold calls, cold email, LinkedIn outreach |
| Skill emphasis | Speed of response, qualification rigor | Research depth, resilience, relationship-building |
| Real-world example | GitLab's public handbook assigns inbound lead flow to its SDR role | GitLab's public handbook assigns outbound prospecting to its BDR role |
| Career path | The more common default title; leads to AE | Often an entry-level rung before SDR or AE, per Salesforce |

GitLab's publicly published sales handbook splits the roles exactly this way: SDRs manage inbound lead flow, while BDRs run outbound prospecting and territory-based outreach — a real, named example of the dominant industry convention in practice, not a theoretical split.

This structural separation allows for specialization: SDRs become experts at rapid qualification and nurturing, BDRs become experts at research and cold outreach. But it only works if the organization has enough volume to justify both roles. According to LinkedIn Sales Solutions, unclear role definition is a top-three reason B2B sales reps leave within their first year, contributing to a 10-15% annual voluntary attrition rate — a strong argument for defining SDR/BDR scope precisely in the job posting, not just picking whichever title sounds more senior.

A BDR limited to inbound will underperform because they aren't trained in the rapid qualification and nurturing that inbound conversion rewards.

## Which Role Should You Hire First: SDR or BDR?

Hire an SDR first if your business already generates inbound demand — content, paid, SEO — that needs faster qualification. Hire a BDR first if you have little to no inbound flow and need to manufacture pipeline from cold outbound. The choice is a direct read of your pipeline health and marketing maturity, not which role is "better."

The real question is which bottleneck is hurting revenue more. If you have leads but aren't converting them quickly enough, you need an SDR. If you have no leads at all, you need a BDR.

### Signs You Need an SDR First

- **Inbound volume is high:** Marketing is generating more than 20-30 qualified inbound leads a month and your AEs can't keep up — the bottleneck is qualification speed, not lead generation.
- **Response time is slow:** Leads go cold because no one is dedicated to following up within minutes of a form fill.
- **Attribution is already clear:** You understand which channels drive inbound leads, as covered in our guide to [marketing attribution modeling](https://marketing.growthgear.com.au/seo/what-is-marketing-attribution-modeling-explained), and you need someone to work those known sources.
- **AEs are wasting time:** Account Executives spend too much time on unqualified calls instead of closing.

HubSpot's State of Sales research found that full-cycle reps who prospect and close themselves spend only 34% of their time actually selling, compared with 45%+ for reps supported by a dedicated SDR or BDR function — evidence that splitting the role, whichever you hire first, pays off once volume justifies it.

### Signs You Need a BDR First

- **Inbound volume is low:** Website traffic and content downloads are minimal — there aren't enough inbound leads to justify an SDR's time yet.
- **Category awareness is low:** Buyers don't know your solution exists, common for new categories or unfamiliar brands. Outbound can create awareness while inbound marketing is still being built.
- **AEs have no pipeline:** Account Executives are starving for opportunities and can't close deals they don't have.
- **You can invest in research:** BDR roles ramp slower because cold outbound skill takes longer to develop, but they compound faster once territory research is built.

According to Salesforce's State of Sales research, average B2B quota attainment sits between 54% and 60%, with top performers outperforming peers by up to 67%. A dedicated SDR or BDR raises pipeline volume for AEs, which is one of the levers that moves quota attainment — without one, AEs are often forced to prospect themselves, which drives burnout and inconsistent pipeline.

## How SDR and BDR Roles Fit Into Your Sales Team Structure

SDRs and BDRs both feed the same downstream role, the Account Executive, but as a team scales past 8-10 reps, most B2B organizations split the two functions to separate inbound qualification from outbound prospecting, then unify both under one sales development leader. Below that scale, a single blended role is usually more practical than two half-utilized specialists.

As a sales org grows, the generalist full-cycle model becomes less efficient than specialized roles. SDRs get sharper at nurturing inbound leads, BDRs get sharper at breaking into cold accounts, and each function develops depth that a generalist rarely has time to build.

### What Sales Leaders Are Saying

Sales leaders are genuinely split on whether the SDR/BDR distinction matters in practice. Gail Behun, Senior Director of Revenue Enablement at Juniper Square, put it directly: "There's a lot of interesting discussions between BDRs and SDRs, because a lot of companies don't separate them at all. The company I'm at doesn't separate them. We have inbound SDRs and outbound SDRs, and when I came aboard I said, 'well, aren't the inbound SDRs really BDRs?'"

That view reflects a common reality: plenty of companies use one title, usually SDR, for every top-of-funnel rep regardless of inbound or outbound focus, which simplifies hiring and career pathing but sacrifices some specialization. Other sales leaders push back, arguing that teams which do split the roles see sharper performance in each motion — SDRs focus entirely on speed and qualification, BDRs build the research depth and resilience that cold outreach demands, and neither has to context-switch between reactive and proactive prospecting all day.

Neither camp is universally right. The deciding factor is pipeline volume and team size: enough inbound to keep an SDR fully busy, and enough addressable outbound accounts to keep a BDR productive, is what makes a split structure pay off rather than create two under-utilized roles.

### Aligning Roles with Revenue Goals

Whichever role you hire first, the goal is the same: give Account Executives a steady stream of qualified opportunities. That takes clear communication between sales development, marketing, and sales leadership — marketing supplying quality inbound leads and outbound-ready content, and sales leadership defining qualification criteria and handoff steps in writing rather than leaving them to informal habit.
