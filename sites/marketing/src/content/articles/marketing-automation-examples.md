Most marketing automation advice stays abstract — "nurture your leads," "personalize at scale" — without showing the actual trigger, action, and timing that make a workflow work. The 12 examples below fix that. Each one names the exact trigger, the automated response, and the timing window, so you can build it this week instead of researching it for another month.

**How we selected these examples:** We prioritized workflows with a clearly defined trigger (an action a specific person takes), a specific automated response, and a documented timing window — the three ingredients that separate a working automation from a vague "set up some emails" plan. Each example is grouped by funnel stage — lead generation, retention, and onboarding — because the right workflow to build first depends on where your biggest gap actually sits.

## What Is Marketing Automation, and Why Do Examples Matter?

Marketing automation is software that triggers personalized actions — emails, lead scoring, retargeting, CRM updates — based on predefined rules and prospect behavior, freeing teams from manual, one-by-one follow-up. Seeing the exact trigger, action, and timing of a real workflow is more useful than generic advice, because it shows precisely what to build.

Generic strategy advice tells you to "nurture leads." A workflow example tells you that a pricing-page visit plus a demo request should trigger an instant, personalized sales handoff. According to [HubSpot's 2024 State of Marketing report](https://www.hubspot.com/state-of-marketing), companies using marketing automation generate 451% more qualified leads than those that don't — a gap that comes from consistency, not cleverness.

That gap is about to widen further. [Gartner's 2026 CMO survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-survey-reveals-marketing-leaders-expect-ai-automation-of-marketing-work-to-double-to-36-percent-by-2028) found marketing leaders expect AI-driven automation of marketing work to more than double, from 16% today to 36% by 2028. As Kristina LaRocca-Cerrone, VP Analyst in Gartner's Marketing Practice, put it in that survey: "What's emerging now is a widening gap between CMOs who are still testing use cases, and those who are confident enough to use AI to create real brand differentiation."

Most workflows fall into one of three trigger categories: **behavioral** (a page visit, a cart add, an email click), **transactional** (a purchase, a sign-up, a renewal date), and **temporal** (a set number of days since an event, like 90 days of inactivity). Recognizing which category a gap in your funnel falls into makes it much faster to identify which trigger to build against.

## Lead Generation & Nurture Automation Examples

Lead generation automation systematizes the top-of-funnel path from first touch to a qualified sales handoff. Instead of relying on manual follow-up, these four workflows react to prospect behavior automatically, scoring and routing leads without a rep lifting a finger until the lead is sales-ready.

### Welcome / Lead-Magnet Email Series

**Trigger:** A new subscriber downloads your ebook or signs up for a webinar. **Automated action:** A three-email sequence — the first delivers the promised resource immediately, the second (24 hours later) introduces your value proposition, and the third (72 hours later) proposes a discovery call.

[Omnisend's 2026 email benchmark data](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/email-marketing-statistics/) shows a three-email welcome series generates roughly 90% more orders than a single welcome email. For B2B teams, the same structure translates into faster pipeline engagement rather than direct orders.

### Behavior-Based Lead Scoring + Sales Handoff

**Trigger:** A lead visits your pricing page, downloads a case study, then submits a demo request. **Automated action:** The lead's score crosses a defined threshold (for example, 80 points), triggering an instant assignment to a sales rep along with a summary of the lead's full engagement history.

This scenario — pricing page visit plus demo request equals a sales-qualified lead — is the most common scoring pattern in B2B. For the full point-value model and 30-day rollout plan, see our [inbound marketing automation guide](/content-marketing/inbound-marketing-automation-guide).

### Webinar / Event Follow-Up Sequence

**Trigger:** A prospect registers for a live event but doesn't attend. **Automated action:** A two-part sequence sends a replay link immediately after the event, followed by a related case study and meeting invite 48 hours later. Attendees skip the replay and instead receive a thank-you email with a short survey and a booking link.

### Form Abandonment / Partial Lead Recovery

**Trigger:** A visitor starts filling out a demo-request or pricing form but exits before submitting. **Automated action:** A single, low-pressure email or retargeting ad reminds them of what they were requesting, sent 2-4 hours after the abandoned session, without repeating the full form.

Form abandonment automation catches leads that would otherwise disappear entirely — unlike cart or browse abandonment, there's no second chance without this trigger, since the prospect never provided a way to follow up through any other channel.

The technical requirement is progressive form capture: most form tools only record a submission on the final "Submit" click, so you need a platform that captures partial field data (typically email address, entered first) as soon as it's provided, before the visitor abandons the rest of the form. Without that capability, this workflow can't fire at all — it's worth confirming your form tool supports it before promising this automation to your team.

## E-Commerce & Retention Automation Examples

E-commerce automation converts browsers into buyers and keeps existing customers coming back, triggered by cart behavior, browsing activity, purchase history, and inactivity. These workflows are consistently the highest-ROI automations to build first because they target revenue that's already close to being captured.

### Abandoned Cart Recovery Sequence

**Trigger:** A shopper adds items to their cart but leaves without completing checkout. **Automated action:** A three-email series — a reminder with a clear CTA (1 hour after abandonment), a social-proof message (24 hours later), and a small incentive like a 10% discount (48 hours later).

Per Omnisend's 2026 ecommerce benchmarks, a three-email abandoned cart series performs about 69% better than a single email. For the deeper platform and segmentation playbook, see our [ecommerce marketing automation guide](/content-marketing/ecommerce-marketing-automation-guide).

### Browse Abandonment / Product-View Retargeting

**Trigger:** A visitor views a specific product page (or category) multiple times but never adds it to cart. **Automated action:** A single email or retargeting ad featuring the viewed product, sent within 24 hours, paired with related items rather than a discount — since the shopper hasn't shown purchase intent yet.

Browse abandonment automation reaches a much larger audience than cart abandonment, since most visitors never add anything to a cart at all — but it converts at a lower rate, which is why the follow-up should stay soft rather than lead with a discount.

### Post-Purchase / Cross-Sell Flow

**Trigger:** A customer completes a purchase. **Automated action:** An immediate order-confirmation email, followed three days later by a product recommendation based on purchase history ("customers who bought X also bought Y"), and a feedback request with a complementary discount seven days after that.

The recommendation email works best when it's genuinely tied to the item purchased rather than a generic "you might also like" pull from your bestseller list — a customer who just bought a tent is a far better prospect for a sleeping bag than for an unrelated seasonal promotion. Segment the recommendation logic by product category at minimum, even without a full recommendation engine.

### Win-Back / Re-Engagement Campaign

**Trigger:** A customer hasn't purchased in 90 days (or 120+ days for longer B2B cycles). **Automated action:** A three-email sequence — a "we miss you" message featuring a top-selling product (day 90), a limited-time offer (day 105), and a final incentive-led email (day 120).

Well-run win-back flows typically reactivate a meaningful share of lapsed customers in mature ecommerce programs, based on aggregated ecommerce email benchmark research — the key variable is segmenting by past purchase value and recency rather than sending one generic blast to the entire lapsed list.

## Customer Onboarding & Engagement Automation Examples

Onboarding automation gets new users or customers to their first meaningful result quickly, which is what actually reduces churn — not the number of emails sent. These four workflows trigger off sign-up, purchase, or usage milestones rather than the calendar.

### SaaS / Product Onboarding Sequence

**Trigger:** A user signs up for a free trial or subscription. **Automated action:** A five-touch sequence spanning 21 days — Day 1: welcome and setup tips, Day 3: a feature-highlight video, Day 7: an industry-relevant case study, Day 14: advanced tips, Day 21: an upgrade or renewal offer tied to usage.

The goal throughout is activation — a specific in-product action, like completing a first project or inviting a teammate — not simply reading the emails.

### Renewal / Upsell Trigger Automation

**Trigger:** A subscription is 30 days from expiry, or a customer has used 80% of their plan's capacity. **Automated action:** A personalized email highlighting the value already delivered, with a renewal or upgrade link, escalating to an account-manager call if no action is taken within seven days.

Staggering three touches — 30, 14, and 7 days before expiry — replaces manual churn-tracking spreadsheets with a system sales and success teams can both see.

### Review / Referral Request Automation

**Trigger:** A customer completes a purchase or reaches a usage milestone, such as 30 days active. **Automated action:** A request for a review on your site or a third-party platform like G2, paired with a referral-program invitation, followed by a single reminder seven days later if there's no response.

### Customer Milestone / Anniversary Automation

**Trigger:** A customer reaches a usage or account-anniversary milestone — one year as a customer, 100th order, or a usage-volume threshold. **Automated action:** A personalized thank-you message acknowledging the specific milestone, often paired with a small loyalty reward or an exclusive early-access offer.

Milestone automation is easy to skip because it doesn't drive an immediate transaction, but it's one of the lowest-cost ways to extend customer lifetime value — the trigger is already sitting in your CRM or order history, waiting to be used.

It's also one of the fastest workflows to build in this entire list: the data already exists (signup date, order count), so the only work is defining which milestones qualify and writing one email template. Most teams can launch this workflow inside a single afternoon.

## Which Marketing Automation Workflow Should You Build First?

Build the workflow that touches your highest-volume, highest-drop-off funnel stage first — the single point where fixing one leak prevents the most lost revenue. Start with a workflow that takes less than a day to set up, measure its impact for 30 days, then move to the next-highest-impact gap.

Pull your funnel numbers before choosing: cart-completion rate for ecommerce, demo-to-close rate for B2B, trial-to-paid rate for SaaS. Whichever stage shows the steepest drop is where a single workflow returns the most, and that number — not a generic "best practices" list — should decide your build order. Rebuilding a workflow that's already performing adequately is the single biggest waste of setup time we see in early-stage automation programs.

| Workflow | Setup Effort | Typical Impact | Build First If… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abandoned Cart Recovery | Low (2-3 hrs) | ~69% better than single email | You run ecommerce with cart abandonment above 60% |
| Welcome / Lead-Magnet Series | Low (1-2 hrs) | ~90% more orders vs. single email | You have steady lead-magnet signups but weak follow-through |
| Behavior-Based Lead Scoring | Medium (5-10 hrs) | Feeds the 451% qualified-lead gap | You run a long B2B sales cycle with many inbound leads |
| Browse Abandonment Retargeting | Low (2-3 hrs) | Reaches a larger pool than cart recovery | Most site traffic never reaches checkout |
| Win-Back / Re-Engagement | Medium (3-5 hrs) | Reactivates a meaningful share of lapsed buyers | Your lapsed-customer list is large and unsegmented |
| SaaS Onboarding Sequence | Medium (5-8 hrs) | Lifts trial-to-paid activation | Free-trial signups are healthy but activation is weak |
| Renewal / Upsell Trigger | High (8-12 hrs) | Reduces manual churn-tracking | You run subscriptions with predictable expiry dates |
| Review / Referral Request | Low (1-2 hrs) | Low-cost acquisition lift | You have satisfied customers but few public reviews |

## Grow Your Automation Into a Revenue System

Twelve examples are a starting menu, not a launch checklist. The workflow that matters most is the one tied to your biggest funnel leak — build that one, prove it works, then expand from a position of evidence instead of guesswork.

The teams that get the most out of marketing automation aren't the ones running the most workflows — they're the ones who picked the right first workflow, measured it honestly, and only added complexity once the first one was proven. Start narrow, and let the data tell you what to build next.
